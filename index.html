About these images

Observations, estimates and forecasts

An observation is what an instrument measured at a given time. An estimate is a quantity derived from observations with a model or algorithm, such as rainfall rate from satellite data. A forecast predicts the future. Everything shown in this version of Horizonte is an observation from Meteosat, turned into an image. There are no estimates, forecasts, detections or warnings.

Where the images come from

The images come from EUMETSAT, the European operational satellite agency, through its public EUMETView Web Map Service. They are taken by the Flexible Combined Imager on Meteosat-12 (MTG-I1), which scans the full Earth disc every 10 minutes from a geostationary orbit about 36,000 km above 0° longitude. EUMETSAT classifies these image products as Core data, free to reuse under the CC BY 4.0 licence with attribution.

How Horizonte prepares them

  1. Once an hour, an automated job asks EUMETView which observation times are available.
  2. It downloads a crop of the region for a few hours of observations (one frame every 20 minutes) for each layer, plus a coastline and border overlay.
  3. It checks every file and records the observation times in a manifest, then publishes the files as a static website.
  4. Your browser only downloads these prepared images. Visiting the page sends no requests to EUMETSAT.

Limitations

  • These are images for viewing, not measurements. Colours are stretched and compressed (JPEG), so pixel values cannot be converted back to temperatures or reflectances. Scientific analysis needs the original numerical data, available from the EUMETSAT Data Store.
  • The time shown is the nominal start of the scan. Each full-disc scan takes about 10 minutes, so places in the image were observed a few minutes apart.
  • Images are reprojected to a latitude/longitude grid. The satellite sees this region at a slant from above the equator, so high clouds appear shifted away from it, towards the north, by roughly 10 km or more (parallax).
  • Updates are hourly and EUMETView publishes images with a delay, so the newest frame is normally 20 to 90 minutes old. Older data is flagged on the page.
  • The region does not include the Canary Islands.
